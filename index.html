Why Einstein's Field Equations Breakdown at Quantum Scale

The fundamental conflict between General Relativity (GR) and Quantum Mechanics (QM).

General Relativity (Einstein)

Macro / Smooth
G_μν + Λg_μν = (8πG / c⁴) T_μν

Treats spacetime as a smooth, continuous 4D geometric manifold. Energy and momentum (T_μν) dictate the exact curvature (G_μν) of space. It is strictly deterministic and continuous down to infinitely small distances.

Quantum Mechanics (Planck/Schrödinger)

Micro
iℏ ∂Ψ/∂t = ĤΨ

Treats the universe as discrete quanta and probability amplitude waves. Quantum uncertainty (Δx Δp ≥ ℏ/2) creates microscopic zero-point energy fluctuations.

Why Einstein's Field Equations Break Down
  • Black Hole Singularities: GR predicts infinitely dense singularity points where spacetime curvature becomes infinite ($\infty$), resulting in mathematical divide-by-zero errors.
  • Planck Scale Quantum Foam: At distances near the Planck length ($\sim 10^{-35}\text{m}$), quantum fluctuations warp geometry into unpredictable foam, violating GR's assumption of smooth geometry.
  • Non-Renormalizability: Applying quantum field theory perturbative math to gravity yields infinite probabilities that cannot be cancelled out by standard normalization.
